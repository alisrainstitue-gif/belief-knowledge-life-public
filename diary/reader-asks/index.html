From notes recorded in September and October 2026

The reader who asks

  • Reading the Qur’an

In Fatiha, I am the one asking.

That changes how I read the opening. Allah Subhanahu wa Taala is giving me words to say, but the request is from my side. I begin in His name, invoking Ar-Rahman and Ar-Raheem. I praise Him, acknowledge Him as Rabb, and recognise Him as the Master of the Day of Judgment. Before I ask for guidance, I have already said something about whom I worship, where I seek help, and what I believe about the end of this life.

Then comes the request: اهدنا الصراط المستقيم. Guide us to the straight path. Not a path described only through an abstract definition, either. I ask for the path of people He has blessed, not those under anger or those who have gone astray. There are people in this request. I want to be among some, and not among others. Fatiha 1:1–7

It reminds me of the opening pages of a book that explain whom the book is for. Here, though, the reader is speaking. The reader already has an orientation and a need.

And then Baqarah begins by describing the book as guidance for the Muttaqeen.

This is the connection I want to stay with. In Fatiha, someone asks. In Baqarah, the book is introduced, and the people for whom it is guidance are described. The reader's side and the response from Allah Subhanahu wa Taala seem to meet. Belief in the Day of Judgment is there in Fatiha; certainty about the Akhirah, the life to come, appears in Baqarah. Worship is there in Fatiha; salah appears in Baqarah. I am not saying these are identical expressions. I am trying to see what carries from one passage into the other. Baqarah 2:2–5

The Akhirah matters to the whole picture. If a human life does not end where I usually stop counting it, success and failure cannot mean quite the same thing. Nor can knowledge mean only what I can establish from the immediately visible world. Revelation, including what came before, belongs to the description of these people too.

Two words also caught my attention: yu'minoon and yooqinoon. Believing, and being certain. I was hearing a quality of trust in the first and a firmness of conviction in the second. Why is that second word used specifically with the Akhirah? What would it mean for the end of life to be that convincing in a person's understanding, rather than merely something they agree will happen?

The spending also mattered to this reading. Rizq includes intellect, among other things. The verse says that they spend from what they have been given. It does not specifically say zakat, or giving to the poor. I was understanding this as spending or using what has been provided.

Then the passage says that these people are upon guidance from their Rabb. That is not quite the same wording as guidance for them. They believe, establish salah, spend, and have certainty about the Akhirah. They already seem to be doing the right things. What, then, is this description of being upon guidance telling me?

I had returned to Fatiha because of the groups. Now I also want to understand the person making the request.
