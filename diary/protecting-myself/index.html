Entry 8

From notes recorded in September and October 2026

What am I trying to protect?

  • Reading the Qur’an
  • Belief and conduct

I needed to be clear about why I wanted psychology, philosophy and neuroscience in this inquiry.

I am not trying to use modern science to prove the Quran. I am trying to understand the Quran with the knowledge available in the time in which I live. We have learned things about human beings, their brains, their choices and their relationships. Some of that knowledge will be sound; some may change. I want to use it to ask better questions of the immense wisdom I believe is here.

The distinction matters when choosing studies. An experiment can examine how people respond to a reward they can receive now or later. It cannot reproduce the Akhirah in a laboratory. I know that. I do not need the comparison abandoned for failing to be the very thing it could never experimentally contain. I want to know whether the study illuminates a process relevant to my question, and how.

For example, how does a person's understanding of their future enter present judgment? How do accepted sources of knowledge affect what feels true, urgent or morally important? What happens when someone has already rejected a position and is then asked to reconsider it? The passage begins by describing rejection; the research question should not forget that context and treat the person as if they had no prior orientation.

Likewise, professing a belief and allowing it to organise conduct are not the same question. I wanted research into the actual difference: people saying one thing, behaving differently under other conditions, and the mechanisms involved. A general caution that belief does not guarantee goodness does not do that work.

My interest in brain mechanisms is not new, but I still have much to learn about psychological theories and philosophy. I want the learning to be useful and lean. I do not need to restart my education from the definition of research. I need methods suited to this kind of reading, and knowledge that helps me examine the questions properly.

Most importantly, I am not doing this so I can classify the people around me.

I want to protect myself from becoming like the latter two descriptions. If repeated choices, ways of thinking, or environments can leave a person less able or less willing to recognise something, I want to understand what to watch for. If a stated belief can remain separate from conduct, I want to understand that separation in practical terms.

The question reaches parenting too. What should we be attentive to while a person's habits and understanding are developing? What are we encouraging without noticing? What would help someone become truthful and receptive rather than merely able to give the right answer?

Those are the kinds of questions I want the research to open. Not a diagnosis of the people in the verses, and not a verdict on someone I know. I am concerned with choices and the formation of conduct, not with treating an illness or an atypical brain as a moral description.

The request for guidance includes a wish to be among certain people. Understanding their descriptions is not only an intellectual exercise. I want to know what, in the life I am actually living, I need to do differently.
