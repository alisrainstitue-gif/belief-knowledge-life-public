When Everything Became a Question Mark · Chapter One · Entry 06

I Thought Philosophy Was Something Philosophers Did

11 October 2026·English

I did not decide to study philosophy.

I was trying to work out whether a psychology lecture had actually proved what I thought it had proved.

That was all.

So I started searching.

Not for philosophy.

For questions.

If the brain affects the mind, does that mean the mind is only the brain?

If evolution explains biological complexity, what does that actually tell us about God?

What can science establish, and where does interpretation begin?

I would follow one question, open something else, find another term I did not know, and follow that too.

And I kept ending up in the same place.

Philosophy.

Philosophy of science.

Philosophy of mind.

Then epistemology appeared.

I had to look that one up.

It was almost funny.

For years, philosophy had existed somewhere in the background of my education as a subject other people studied. Philosophers asked enormous questions about reality, knowledge and existence.

I worked with data.

Or at least that was how I had divided the world in my head.

But I was not reading philosophy because I had suddenly become interested in abstract questions.

I was trying to answer very practical ones that had already entered my life.

How do I know when evidence supports a conclusion?

What exactly is a human mind?

What am I assuming before I even begin answering either of those questions?

Somewhere in that process, philosophy stopped looking like an optional subject.

I had been asking philosophical questions before I knew what to call them.
