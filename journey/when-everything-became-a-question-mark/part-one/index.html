When Everything Became a Question Mark · Part One

When Knowledge Became Harder to Trust

30 September 2026·English

For a while, learning Deen felt like finding a place for a part of me that had been looking for one.

Science still mattered to me, but career achievement by itself had stopped feeling like enough. Learning Deen was different. It was intellectually alive, personally meaningful, and it opened the possibility that the skills I already had could serve something I cared about more deeply.

I also carried an expectation that I had never really examined.

If someone spends years studying truth, justice, accountability, humility and our relationship with Allah Subhanahu wa Taala, surely some of that knowledge should become visible in the person.

Then I came close enough to people I hoped to learn from for that assumption to become uncomfortable.

It was not that I expected perfection. They were ordinary human beings, like you and me. But some of what I saw was difficult to place beside what was being taught. At first I tried to separate the two: the knowledge could still be right even if the person was flawed.

Yet another question had already entered.

When we learn from someone, do we receive only the information? Or can the person carrying it also shape what is emphasised, how it is interpreted, and what it looks like when lived?

I had gone looking for knowledge.

I had not expected to come away wondering what else travels with it.

And I did not yet know that the same question was about to follow me somewhere I trusted for a very different reason.
