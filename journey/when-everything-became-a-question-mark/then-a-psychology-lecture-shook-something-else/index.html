When Everything Became a Question Mark · Chapter One · Entry 03

Then a Psychology Lecture Shook Something Else

2 October 2026·English

For the next day or two after one psychology lecture, I was genuinely afraid that something I believed might not survive what I had just learned.

The timing could hardly have been worse.

I was already disappointed by some of the people I had trusted to teach me Deen. Then I started a psychology course, something much closer to the world I already knew and trusted.

Science.

The lecture began with the brain, evolution and natural selection.

I was completely comfortable there.

Then we moved to biological complexity.

The professor discussed the old watchmaker argument: if you found something as complex as a watch, you would assume someone had made it. So when we look at something far more complex, like life itself, does that point to a designer?

A Creator?

Then came the other side.

Natural selection could produce complexity without each part being deliberately designed.

Some features of living organisms did not seem particularly well designed.

And if complexity needed a Creator, then who created the Creator?

The lecture kept moving.

From evolution, to complexity, to design, to God, and then into the mind and the brain.

The mind could be understood through the physical brain. Human thought, emotion and behaviour could be understood through biology and evolution.

And while I was listening, none of this felt confusing or disconnected.

Each point seemed to follow naturally from the one before it.

The explanations were coherent.

The reasoning made sense.

I was not sitting there resisting any of it.

I was learning.

And when the lecture finished, I was left with something I had not expected.

Fear.

For years, one of the things I had valued most about science was exactly this: evidence did not have to protect what I already believed. If I was wrong, I wanted to know.

Suddenly that principle became frightening.

What if this was what the evidence actually showed?

What if evolution really did remove the need for a Creator?

What if the mind was simply what the brain did?

What did that mean for the soul?

And then an even more disturbing thought came.

What if I believed these things only because I did not understand the science well enough?

That stayed with me.

I was already struggling with the people I had trusted to explain Deen to me.

Now science seemed to be questioning the beliefs themselves.

For those one or two days, I did not have an intellectual problem to solve.

I was simply scared.

Scared that if I kept following the evidence honestly, I might end up losing something I had never imagined I could lose.

Was this useful?
