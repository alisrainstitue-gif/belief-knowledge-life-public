When Everything Became a Question Mark · Part Two

When Evidence Became Harder to Read

30 September 2026·English

Science had always felt different.

I did not need the person explaining a result to be morally exemplary before I could take the result seriously. Evidence, method, testing and correction were supposed to carry the weight.

That was precisely why the next discomfort unsettled me more.

As I kept learning, I began noticing moments when the evidence seemed to establish one thing, while the explanation that followed reached much further. A finding about the world could become a statement about what a human being is, what reality contains, or which kinds of explanation should count at all.

The transition was easy to miss.

The same subject continued. The same confident voice continued. The scientific language continued. Unless I stopped and asked what had actually been shown, an interpretation could arrive with the authority I had already given the evidence.

So I started slowing things down.

What was observed? What was inferred? What connected the two? Was that connecting step itself scientific, philosophical, or something else?

The questions did not make me want science less. They made me want to understand it more carefully.

But by then the discomfort had spread. Questions about Deen, teachers, evidence, interpretation and authority were beginning to meet each other in my head. I could no longer place them neatly in separate rooms.

And then, surrounded by people one day, I realised how alone I felt with them.

The hardest possibility came after that.

What if I was the common factor?

What if I had misunderstood the science? What if my expectations of knowledge were wrong? What if I was noticing other people's assumptions while remaining blind to my own?

For a while, everything became a question mark.

And the question that stayed was the one I still need this journey to answer:

Where are they right? Where am I wrong?
