When Everything Became a Question Mark · Chapter One · Entry 02

Getting Closer Changed the View

2 October 2026·English

Finding a place where my skills could serve something I believed in felt almost too neat.

I was no longer only attending classes or listening to teachers from a distance. I was working with some of them.

That changed something.

From a classroom, you mostly meet a person's knowledge. Working together brings you closer to everything around it: how commitments are handled, how decisions are made, what happens when people disagree, how responsibility is shared, and what becomes important when things are inconvenient.

At first, I did not think much of the smaller things.

A deadline would pass. Something agreed upon would remain unfinished. A responsibility would quietly move to someone else. Policies that had seemed sensible in discussion became harder to follow when they required people to put shared commitments, team responsibilities and accountability ahead of individual preferences.

None of these things, on their own, seemed dramatic.

People are busy. People forget. People have different working styles. And I certainly did not expect someone who teaches Deen to stop being an ordinary human being.

But over time, the pattern became harder for me to dismiss.

What unsettled me was not simply that people made mistakes. It was the distance I sometimes felt between the principles being taught and the way ordinary working relationships were being handled.

I had entered that work believing that my role was fairly simple. They had knowledge I did not have. I could help with the infrastructure around it: systems, technology, management, policies and the practical work needed to help that knowledge reach further.

I had assumed the difficult part would be building the infrastructure.

Instead, I began discovering how difficult it could be to build anything that required people to accept limits, keep commitments, answer difficult questions or remain accountable to a system larger than themselves.

And that left me with a discomfort I did not yet know how to name.

I still respected the knowledge.

I still wanted to learn.

But getting closer had changed the view.
