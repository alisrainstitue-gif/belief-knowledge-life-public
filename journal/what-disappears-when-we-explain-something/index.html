Reading with Questions · Chapter Two · Entry 01

What Disappears When We Explain Something?

11 October 2026·English

The question had followed me out of a psychology lecture: what if evolution really did remove the need for a Creator?

For a day or two, I had been scared of where following the evidence might take me. I could understand how life might become complex through natural selection. What troubled me was what seemed to follow: if we knew how, what reason was left to believe?

Richard Dawkins wrote in The Blind Watchmaker: “Darwin made it possible to be an intellectually fulfilled atheist.”

I began going back over the argument, trying to separate what the science explained from what I was being asked to conclude. I carried that question with me when I opened the Qur’an.

In the opening of al-Baqarah, I encountered ghayb, the unseen, alongside descriptions of what people believed and did. Then I reached a passage where warning people or not warning them made no difference: لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ, “they do not believe.”

The next verse began خَتَمَ اللَّهُ. Allah Subhanahu wa Taala has sealed.

Their hearts. Their hearing.

I was thinking about their choices. Psychology could help me understand how repeated responses become habits and how commitments can make contrary evidence harder to accept.

So why did it not say that they had sealed themselves?

The One named as sealing was Allah Subhanahu wa Taala.

I could keep following the human choices, the habits, the possible chain of consequences. None of that would change who was named in the sentence. What I was beginning to see was a reality in which human choice and divine agency belonged together.

I had feared that understanding a process might leave no room for Allah Subhanahu wa Taala.

Why should knowing how it happened mean it happened without Him?
